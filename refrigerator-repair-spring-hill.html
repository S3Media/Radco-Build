Know the Warning Signs

Warning Signs Your Refrigerator Needs Professional Service

1

Uneven Temperatures or Warm Spots Inside

Food spoils faster in one area than another, usually pointing to airflow problems, a failing fan, or thermostat issues.

2

Excessive Frost or Ice Buildup

A classic sign of a faulty defrost system, door seal, or evaporator fan. Left unchecked, it can damage the compressor.

3

Water Pooling on the Floor or Inside

Often caused by a clogged drain tube, cracked water line, or worn inlet valve.

4

Strange Noises — Buzzing, Clicking, or Grinding

These typically come from the compressor, condenser fan, or start relay and should never be ignored.

5

Ice Maker or Water Dispenser Stops Working

Problems here usually trace back to the valve, supply line, or the ice mechanism itself.

6

Constant Running or Short Cycling

Your fridge works overtime, driving up your electric bill and wearing out components faster.

Make the Smart Call

Should You Repair or Replace Your Refrigerator?

This is one of the most common questions we hear. The honest answer depends on three factors: the age of your unit, the cost of repair versus replacement, and how much longer you want reliable performance. Most quality refrigerators last 10 to 15 years with proper care, according to guidance from the U.S. Department of Energy.

If your unit is under 10 years old and the repair cost is less than half the price of a comparable new model, repairing almost always makes better financial sense. New refrigerators can easily run $800 to $2,500, while many common repairs fall well below that threshold.

We also factor in energy efficiency. An older model that still cools properly after repair may continue to perform efficiently for years, especially if you keep the condenser coils clean and maintain proper temperatures (the U.S. Food and Drug Administration recommends 40°F or below for the refrigerator section). Our technicians give you a clear, no-pressure recommendation based on your specific situation. Many Spring Hill customers are surprised how much life remains in their current refrigerator after a professional repair.

Keep It Running

Prevention Tips That Extend Refrigerator Life

A little routine care prevents most emergency calls. Here’s what we recommend:

Dust the condenser coils at the bottom or back of the unit every 3–6 months.
Check and clean door gaskets so they seal tightly.
Avoid overloading shelves — good airflow matters.
Keep the temperature at 37–40°F in the refrigerator and 0°F in the freezer.
Schedule an annual maintenance check — we catch small issues before they become expensive problems.
